About FINTECH

We're building a borderless economy

Financial tools should be powerful, transparent and available to everyone — not just to the people who already have a private banker.

Our journey

Four years, one stubborn idea

Click through the milestones that got us from a frustrated group chat to a licensed payments platform.

The beginning

A group chat about a $47 wire fee

Our founders were splitting the cost of a shared project across three countries and lost nearly a hundred dollars to intermediary bank fees on a single transfer. Nobody could explain where the money went.

Six weeks later they had a prototype that priced the same transfer at zero, and a very short list of people willing to try it.

  • Company incorporated with a team of four
  • First closed beta with 800 early users
  • Seed round raised to fund licensing work
Team size
4 people
Public launch

Opening the doors, and the rails

We shipped the public app, secured our Electronic Money Institution authorisation, and connected to local payment rails in our first twelve markets — which is when transfers stopped taking days and started taking seconds.

The waitlist cleared in under a month.

  • EMI authorisation granted
  • Virtual and physical cards launched
  • 100,000 accounts opened in year one

Going global

Thirty-eight countries and a business account

Demand from freelancers and small teams pushed us to build multi-user accounts, batch payouts and accounting exports. Business became our fastest-growing product within two quarters.

We also passed our first SOC 2 Type II audit — the unglamorous work that makes the rest possible.

  • Business accounts and batch payouts shipped
  • SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 certified
  • Coverage expanded to 38 countries

Our mission

Make the honest option the easy one

Most financial products are priced to reward inattention. A rate that drifts, a fee that appears on the statement rather than the screen, a transfer that takes three days for no technical reason at all.

We think the opposite should be true: the clearest product should also be the cheapest and the fastest. Every decision here gets measured against that.

Radical clarity

If a customer needs a glossary to understand a price, the price is wrong.

Access as default

The best tools shouldn't be reserved for the largest balances.
